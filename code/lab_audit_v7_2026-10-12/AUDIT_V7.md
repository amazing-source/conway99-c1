## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

Every claim I recomputed or re-derived is correct, and I found no error of substance. The two "new" graphs are real:
they are strongly regular with parameters (243,22,1,2), pairwise non-isomorphic and different from the two graphs
known before, with automorphism groups of order 864 and 3888. The campaign states its own limits correctly: no
asymmetric graph was obtained, and the questions it calls open are open. What I could not re-verify are the results
that rest on solver runs of the cloud session (listed in section 3) and the external dossiers that are not in the
repository.

## 4. Small inconsistencies (no consequence found)

* Note 165 s.3 states condition (L) with two types of non-adjacent pairs (6/0 and 3/3); note 175 s.1 finds three
  admissible multisets (rotations twice each, S3 once each, the three reflections twice each). The linear form of
  165 s.6 does not separate the first and the third, and the argument "no 3/3 pair implies a coboundary" still holds,
  but the statement of 165 should be aligned on 175.
* The data README says "all four known graphs are 3-covers of BH along a regular spread": true, and it is also the
  reason why every regularity observed on these four graphs may be an artefact of the construction, as notes 176 and
  177 themselves warn.

## 5. What the campaign establishes about asymmetry, and what it does not

* Established (given section 3): the four graphs are covers of BH with a deck group Z3, so their groups are divisible
  by 3; the smallest group found is 864.
* Not established: that every srg(243,22,1,2) has a regular spread, or that every regular-spread graph is a Z3-cover.
  The two doors to an asymmetric graph at 243 are therefore (i) an S3-cover of BH, (ii) a graph with no regular spread.
  Neither is known to exist; neither is excluded.
* For Conway's 99 nothing transfers as an obstruction: no regular spread exists there (lemma 1), so no symmetry floor
  of this kind.
